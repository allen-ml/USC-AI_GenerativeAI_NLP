Course Overview

Welcome to TAC 459: Generative AI and Natural Language Processing, the capstone course of the USC Artificial Intelligence Minor at the University of Southern California. Co-instructed by Allen Bolourchi and Reza Jafarkhani, the course connects technical foundations, industry analysis, and hands-on AI product development through three complementary components.

Technical Foundations. Students explore how generative AI works and how frontier models are built, adapted, and evaluated. Topics include transformers and attention, prompting, RAG, efficient fine-tuning with LoRA and QLoRA, alignment, RLHF and PPO, model evaluation and benchmarks, and AI safety. Hands-on work connects these concepts to many use cases including conversational AI, text generation and summarization, machine translation, and multimodal applications.

AI Company Overview. Midway through the semester, teams investigate emerging AI companies backed by YC or elite VCs. Students analyze the products, underlying technology, customers, market opportunity, and business models, then present the company and a concrete GenAI use case to the class.

AI Capstone Product. Over nine weeks, teams apply class concepts across the AI product lifecycle: ideation, a shared minimum viable product, multiple prototypes, and a complete application built with APIs to frontier models. Students begin with course code and progressively use AI-assisted coding tools to build and refine their products. The course culminates in a working product demonstration and a startup-style launch pitch connecting the technical architecture with the user problem, product value, and business opportunity.

AI Company Overview

Midsemester industry case study

The AI Company Overview connects the technical material with the products and businesses being built around generative AI. Teams investigate an emerging company, explain why its technology matters, and use that analysis to inform their own product ideas.

Presentation: November 2, 2026 · 12:00 PM · In person

AI Company Overview presentation upload (presentation day only)

Select an emerging company

Form a team of six; four teams may have seven members. Select a company at the pre-seed, seed, Series A, or Series B stage with an emerging GenAI application. Y Combinator companies are preferred; other selections require professor approval. Discuss the choice with the professor before preparing the overview.

Explain the product and technology

Cover the problem, product, generative AI technology, target customers, practical use case, differentiation, and market opportunity. Include one or two concrete GenAI examples so the class can understand how the product works and what makes it distinctive.

Present and connect to your product

Prepare a 10-minute company overview and a two-minute Q&A, with every team member participating. Add one slide and a one-minute pitch for each proposed capstone product idea. Upload the presentation to Brightspace. Teams are also encouraged to contact a founder, executive, or employee; a 15–20 minute guest segment is optional if the company participates.

AI Capstone Product

Full course roadmap (PDF) →

The AI Capstone Product turns course concepts into a working application over nine weeks. The same team moves from a problem and product idea to one shared minimum viable product (MVP), develops multiple prototypes, and selects a direction to refine into the complete product. Each product must use a frontier-model API for at least one feature.

Class concepts & ideas→One team MVP→Multiple prototypes→Complete product
